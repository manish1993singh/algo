**Problem Statement: The Staircase Climb**

A runner is standing at the bottom of a staircase with $n$ steps. From any given step, the runner can take either $1$ step or $2$ steps upward.

Determine the total number of distinct ways the runner can reach the top of the staircase (step $n$).

---

**Input Format**

* The first line contains an integer $t$, representing the number of test cases.
* Each of the next $t$ lines contains a single integer $n$, representing the total number of steps in the staircase for that test case.

---

**Constraints**

* $1 \le t \le 100$
* $1 \le n \le 45$

---

**Output Format**

* For each test case, print a single integer representing the total number of distinct ways to reach the $n$-th step on a new line.

---

**Sample Input 0**

```text
2
2
3

```

**Sample Output 0**

```text
2
3

```

**Explanation 0**

* **For $n = 2$:** There are two distinct ways to reach the 2nd step:
1. $1 \text{ step} + 1 \text{ step}$
2. $2 \text{ steps}$


* **For $n = 3$:** There are three distinct ways to reach the 3rd step:
1. $1 \text{ step} + 1 \text{ step} + 1 \text{ step}$
2. $1 \text{ step} + 2 \text{ steps}$
3. $2 \text{ steps} + 1 \text{ step}$
